# State

Present tense: what exists, what is in flight, what waits on the owner.

## What exists

Isopix 1.0: a public repo (`github.com/pdxfixit/isopix`, MIT) whose history starts at release 1.0.1 on 2026-10-13. It was built from 2026-10-12 as Isogrid, from the owner's [design brief](design-brief.md), and lived at its old address; that history is in a private, archived repo. The site is https://isopix.app (since 2026-10-15; isopix.pdxfixit.com is its old address). Hosting: [deploy-cloudflare.md](deploy-cloudflare.md).

**Engine** (`web/engine/`; `test/` holds the `node --test` suites):

- Lattice math: coordinates, hit test, children/parent, the six cells around a vertex, edge neighbors, hexagon distance, the three line families, edges and their refine/coarsen. The brief's verified math is proven by tests, including its three prototype checks.
- The cell model with the one-cell-or-children invariant, `sub` counts, Keep fine detail / Paint behind / Replace, and a diff-based undo log that also drives previews (rollback) and time-lapse replay.
- Brushes: triangle, rhombus, hexagon, trapezoid, big triangle, drag-sized face, drag-sized cube, stamp. Line Strip and Edge. Cube-face tags.
- Fill over stored cells (never 4ᵏ tiny cells), bounded on empty paper; past 40,000 cells it fills bigger cells.
- Papers: each drawing keeps its own (light or dark), saved with it; the inks follow it (`engine/paper.js`).
- Symmetry: the exact 12-element group around a vertex (mirror, 6-fold, both), faces follow.
- Selection regions, density change both ways (Crisp, Blend in Oklab, snap to palette), hidden children (and bold edges) restored on refine until painted on, marquee snapping, stamps that scale with the level.
- SVG export (merged polygons with holes), project files (shape-checked on load).

**App** (`web/`): the canvas with fading gutters and finer-level hints; tools, brush picker, palette with auto-shade and a custom color, the chunked level slider of hexagons (a rail hanging under the top buttons on phones, a top bar from 768 px) with follow-zoom and lock; pan and zoom (pinch, Ctrl+wheel, trackpad pinch, wheel pans, and the zoom rail on the left edge: zoom in, a log-scale slider, zoom out, ⌖ Fit drawing; `test/view.test.mjs`, `just look zoom fit`); a palette whose add-color and auto-shade buttons stay pinned while the inks scroll; on short landscape screens one bottom row; the selection bar (Finer, Coarser, Crisp/Blend, Snap, Save as stamp, Erase, Done), the density handle with a notch track, and the experimental pinch-inside and long-press-then-pinch gestures; the top bar's Drawing button and sheet (name, New, Open, Save drawing, Save picture, SVG, time-lapse; New, Open and Save also in the bar from 700 px) and ⚙️ Settings in folding sections (Drawing, Display, Selection, Learning, About) with value chips; picking a color while the Eraser is on switches to the Pencil; autosave to IndexedDB; manifest and a network-first service worker.

## Try first (for the owner, on the phone)

Once the branch is live:

1. Tap the eye at the foot of the zoom rail: only the drawing and Undo stay. Draw a little, then tap Show tools. Then Settings → Display → Fold the colors: the colors become one round button above the tools; tap it, pick a color, and it folds again. Does the button read as "colors"?
2. Tap the hand at the top of the zoom rail and drag with one finger: the view moves, nothing is drawn. Tap the Pencil to draw again.
3. Rest one finger, then pinch: no dot is left. Lift one finger mid-pinch and keep moving the other: it keeps panning.
4. On an iPhone or iPad: Drawing → Save drawing opens the share sheet; Save to Files. On a computer in Chrome: Save asks where once, then Save (or Ctrl+S) writes the same file.
5. Tap the square tile at the start of the colors: pick Sunset, then pick two colors with the eyedropper. Both should stay, in a palette called My Sunset. Is the tile easy to spot?

Before that:

1. Draw at a big notch, pinch-zoom in, and draw again: does the level following zoom feel natural, and is the lock easy to find?
2. Drag a cube, select around it, and drag the round handle up and down: does the density change read clearly, and does Coarser then Finer bring the detail back?
3. Slide the zoom rail on the left (under the Drawing button): about 16 px of slide doubles the zoom. Is that too fast? + and − double and halve it; ⌖ at its foot fits the drawing on screen.
4. Turn the phone sideways: the palette and tools share one row; the zoom rail sits in the top-left corner and the size rail under the top buttons. Is anything hard to reach?
5. Settings → Drawing → Paper: flip a drawing to dark paper and draw with the light inks; then start a New drawing and check it opens on the paper matching the phone's mode.
